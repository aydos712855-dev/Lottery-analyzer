Data management
How LottoLens works
LottoLens is a statistical analysis tool for lottery data. It helps you explore patterns in historical draws and generate number sets using different strategies. It does not predict the future — lottery draws are always independent random events.
Quick start — 3 steps
1
Configure your lottery
Go to Settings and pick a preset (6/49, 6/59, 5/45, 5/70) or enter a custom pool size. This tells the app how many numbers exist in your game.
2
Add past draw results
Go to History and enter past winning numbers. The more draws you add, the more reliable the analysis becomes. You can also import a CSV file for bulk entry.
3
Analyze & generate
Explore the Analysis and Heatmap pages to see patterns, then use Generate to produce number sets with your chosen strategy.
Pages explained
Analysis
The main dashboard. Shows four stat cards at the top (hot count, cold count, overdue count, total draws), a frequency bar chart for the top 15 most-drawn numbers, a hot/cold/overdue ball display, and a pair frequency table showing which two numbers appear together most often.
Heatmap
A grid of every number in the pool, color-coded from cold blue (rarely drawn) to hot orange (frequently drawn). Hover any cell to see its exact count and how many draws ago it last appeared. Below the grid, the gap chart ranks numbers by how long they have been absent.
Generate
Pick a strategy, set how many number sets you want (1–10) and how many numbers per set (5–7), then click Generate sets. Each result card shows the numbers and their sum (Σ), which helps you spot sum-range balance. Results animate in one by one.
History
Your draw database. Add results manually with a date and space-separated numbers, delete any row with the × button, or bulk-import from a CSV file. All analysis updates instantly as you add or remove draws.
Settings
Configure the lottery pool (max number and pick count), adjust the thresholds that define hot/cold/overdue, and manage your data (export to JSON or CSV, import JSON, or reset everything).
Generation strategies
🔥
Hot picks
Draws from the pool of numbers that have appeared most frequently across all recorded draws. The assumption is that frequently drawn numbers may continue to appear. Works best with 30+ draws in history.
❄️
Cold picks
Draws from the least frequent numbers. The reasoning is the gambler's intuition that underrepresented numbers are "due" to appear — though statistically each draw is independent and this carries no predictive weight.
⚖️
Balanced mix
Combines roughly equal portions of hot numbers, cold numbers, and overdue numbers into each set. Aims to cover a spread of statistical categories, resulting in more varied sets than pure hot or cold strategies.
⏱️
Overdue picks
Focuses on numbers that have not appeared for the longest time (beyond the overdue threshold set in Settings, default 10 draws). If fewer overdue numbers exist than your set size, the remaining slots are filled randomly.
🎲
Pure random
Ignores all historical data and generates truly random selections from the full number pool. Statistically identical to the lottery itself — useful as a baseline or when you have very few draws recorded.
Key concepts
HOT Hot number
A number whose draw count exceeds the pool average by more than the hot threshold (default ×1.3). Threshold is adjustable in Settings.
COLD Cold number
A number drawn less than the cold threshold fraction of the pool average (default ×0.5). Very infrequent relative to other numbers.
OVERDUE Overdue number
A number that has not appeared in the last N draws (default 10). A number can be both cold and overdue, or hot and overdue if it was frequently drawn but then disappeared.
Pair frequency
How often any two specific numbers have appeared in the same draw together. A high pair frequency simply means they coincidentally appeared together often — it does not mean they are linked.
Set sum (Σ)
The total of all numbers in a generated set. In a 6/49 draw the sum of winning numbers typically falls between 100 and 200. Very low or very high sums are statistically less common, so the Σ display helps you assess balance at a glance.
Importing draw data
To bulk-import past draws, prepare a plain text or CSV file where each line is one draw in the format:
2025-01-04, 5 12 21 31 38 47
2025-01-08, 7 14 21 28 35 42
2025-01-15, 3 11 21 33 40 47
The first column is the date (any format), the remaining columns are the numbers. Numbers can be separated by spaces, commas, or semicolons. Duplicate numbers within a row are automatically removed. Rows with fewer than 2 valid numbers are skipped. After importing, all analysis pages update immediately.
To save your data between sessions, use Settings → Export JSON and keep the file. Next time, use Import JSON to restore everything — draws, config, and thresholds.
A note on randomness
Every legitimate lottery draw is a statistically independent event. No number is ever truly "due," and past frequency has zero influence on future draws. The hot/cold/overdue classifications are descriptive — they tell you what has happened, not what will happen. Use LottoLens to explore data and have fun, not to make financial decisions. Please play responsibly.
